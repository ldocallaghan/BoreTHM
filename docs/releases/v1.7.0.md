# v1.7.0: annulus and friction options, tapered joints, solver corrections and history observations

Prepared for release on 5 November 2026 as `v1.7.0`. The publication status is at the end of this record.

## Changes to the calculation

The options below are all off by default. The two solver corrections apply only to the previous calculation, selected with `hydraulics_model='v1.5.0'`.

### Annulus heat transfer and pressure

`circulating_temperatures` and `hydraulics` accept `annulus_model`:

| Value | Annulus pressure and flow regime | Annulus heat transfer |
|---|---|---|
| `'v1.6.0'` (default) | Bingham plastic with the apparent-viscosity criterion | Dittus-Boelter on the plastic viscosity |
| `'herschel-bulkley'` | Slot model of Founargiotakis et al. (2008), with their transition band | Gnielinski's annulus correlation on the wall viscosity, with laminar values for each wall |
| `'herschel-bulkley-erge'` | The same slot model, with the transition band of Erge et al. (2015) | Gnielinski's annulus correlation on the plastic viscosity, with the same laminar values |

A Herschel-Bulkley mud has a yield stress and a shear stress that rises as a power of the shear rate. The mud's properties are set through `fluid.rheology`, a `fluids.HerschelBulkley` object; a fluid without one is treated as Newtonian. When the yield stress is zero, the annulus is solved directly as a power-law fluid with no iteration. The Herschel-Bulkley values require the v1.6.0 calculation.

### Pipe friction

`circulating_temperatures` accepts `friction='colebrook'` and `roughness`, the absolute wall roughness in metres. The defaults are `friction='blasius'` and `roughness=0.`. Non-zero roughness requires Colebrook. The same friction determines the pressure loss and the heat released into the fluid. The setting applies to internal passages and to the Bingham annulus, where Colebrook on the plastic viscosity is a Newtonian approximation.

In `hydraulics`, `friction` and `roughness` now default to `None`. A supplied circulation result then gives its recorded values, and Blasius with zero roughness applies when no result is supplied. `annulus_model` follows the same rule.

### Drill-pipe joint transitions

`HydraulicJoint(transition='tapered', taper_angle_deg=...)` treats the bore changes at a joint as conical, at the included angle in degrees. The loss coefficients are those of Crane Technical Paper 410, A-26. The annulus side keeps the abrupt treatment, and the cones add no length or wall friction. The default remains `transition='abrupt'`.

### Solver corrections to the previous calculation

Both corrections were already present in the v1.6.0 calculation. They now apply to `hydraulics_model='v1.5.0'` as well, and no equation, tolerance or physical input changes.

- Where the thermal ramps between components overlap, the properties are blended by occupancy, the fraction of a local interval that each component fills. Assemblies with components shorter than 1 m now converge.
- The initial numerical mesh is clipped to the well interval, so that the boundary conditions apply at the physical ends of the well.

The cases that gain from these corrections are those that previously failed to converge. A case on this calculation that already converged and has overlapping ramps, or ramp ends outside the well, can change by a small amount. On synthetic geometries the largest change at the bit or return was 0.0005 °C. No archived result changes: all 467 frozen FORGE samples, the available run-in events and the saved golden tables reproduce. The [development log](../science/model-development.md#1-november-2026-d6-overlapping-ramps-in-the-original-calculation) records the evidence.

### History observations

`wall_temperatures` accepts:

- `observe`, a function that receives a copy of the state at each state boundary and accepted step;
- `radius_m`, a stored radial grid in metres, accepted when each coordinate is within eight units in the last place of the declared grid, a unit being the local floating-point spacing;
- `carry_static_node`, which when `True` starts a static period with no initial fluid-node temperature from the final fluid-node temperature of the preceding static period.

`tool_trip_in` accepts `retain_stage_samples`, which when `True` returns the intermediate samples during stationary circulation, and `T_s0` and `T_f0`, separate initial steel and internal-fluid temperatures in °C.

## Default results

Default results are unchanged from v1.6.0. The result of `circulating_temperatures` gains three entries that record the settings used: `annulus_model`, `friction` and `roughness`. The evidence is in the verification table below.

## FORGE development configuration

The development log compares the model with the FORGE 16A and 16B wells using the settings below. This is a named configuration for those comparisons. It is not a package default, and it carries no recommendation for other wells. The harnesses in `validation/model-development/` pass the options explicitly.

| Setting | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annulus and rheology | `annulus_model='herschel-bulkley-erge'` with no yield stress, and plastic viscosity referenced to 26.7 °C | [Entry 8](../science/model-development.md#31-october-2026-entry-8-score) |
| Pipe friction | `friction='colebrook'`, `roughness=0.` | [Entry 10](../science/model-development.md#31-october-2026-entry-10-sourced-pressure-options) |
| 16A ordinary drill pipe | Body 5.000 by 4.276 in, tool joint 6.625 by 2.750 in, restriction length 25 in | [Entry 12](../science/model-development.md#4-november-2026-entry-12-installed-ordinary-drill-pipe) |
| 16B ordinary drill pipe | Body 5.500 by 4.670 in, tool joint 7.250 by 3.000 in, restriction length 22 in | [Entry 12](../science/model-development.md#4-november-2026-entry-12-installed-ordinary-drill-pipe) |
| Ordinary joint transitions | `transition='tapered'`, `taper_angle_deg=11.5`, with an abrupt annulus | [Entry 13](../science/model-development.md#4-november-2026-entry-13-tapered-drill-pipe-joints) |

The 16B insulated pipe keeps its joints and the abrupt treatment. Heavy-weight drill pipe and the bottom-hole assembly are unchanged.

Entry 13 records these limitations:

- The score changes are mixed. Compared with the abrupt treatment of entry 12, the pressure, hydraulic-power, bit-temperature and return-temperature errors are larger, and the 16B temperature error is smaller.
- The check that scored changes reproduce the predictions tests the implementation. It is not an independent forecast.
- The check against the source's range is limited. No Reynolds range is stated, the Crane formulas were checked through secondary statements, the Tsukano et al. taper length is combined with a bore from a separate patent design, the 5 in angle is transferred to 16B, and a cone approximates a curved profile.
- On the regular-pipe groups of 16B the modelled pressure rise per unit length is 72–90% of the measured rise.
- 16A standpipe pressure remains low by about 1,228 psi as an RMS of the assembly biases, close to its value before these changes.

A near-surface gap also remains. In ten shallow 16B windows, where the partial strings contain no ordinary pipe, the model reads 188–481 psi below the measured standpipe pressure. The tool and surface losses that could account for this are unresolved. At full depth the 16B assemblies read low by 285–375 psi, and the log does not establish that the same losses account for that deficit.
