# Motorless 16B pressure accounting

This diagnostic uses the 45 frozen S1/S2 half-hour samples in EOW assemblies 12, 13, 15 and 16. These assemblies have no documented motor. The revised S2 mud is the primary treatment; the original S1 mud is retained as a separate comparison. Neither treatment is fitted. The original temperature results are preserved.

## Included pressure terms

The existing `model1_coupled.hydraulics` function sums bore friction, bit-nozzle loss and annulus friction, with the signed difference between annulus and bore density columns. The string geometry contains ordinary drill pipe, heavy-weight drill pipe (HWDP) and a length-weighted lower assembly. Each constant-geometry piece uses its midpoint circulating temperature and approximate hydrostatic property pressure. Mud with positive yield point uses the existing Bingham calculation; otherwise the existing Newtonian friction law applies. No tool-joint, surface-line or proprietary steering/MWD restriction term is present. The nozzle discharge coefficient remains 0.95.

The included bore loss is separated into ordinary pipe, HWDP and lower-assembly contributions. HWDP belongs to the BHA subtotal in the six-category presentation. The density-column correction is shown separately, so the reported terms sum exactly to modelled standpipe pressure (SPP). A negative density correction is not frictional heat.

## External estimates, fixed before their calculation

All external calculations use the recorded flow, mud and string lengths, with the unchanged circulating temperatures. Replaying the 90 original/revised cases supplies the temperature profiles and must reproduce the saved S2 pressures and temperature endpoints. No estimate is fed back into a temperature or pressure solve.

1. **Serial BHA passages.** Use every recorded component length, bore and body OD in `component_inputs_s1.csv`, read downwards from the HWDP. Apply the existing Bingham/Blasius pressure-gradient equations to each passage and its annulus. Subtract the existing aggregate lower-assembly losses before adding the serial estimates. The difference is an estimate of geometric aggregation error, and can have either sign. HWDP is already a separate model segment. The bit-body passage is retained separately from its nozzles. A bit's gauge diameter does not describe a sealed cylindrical body blocking the annulus. Over the short bit-body interval, retain the existing aggregate assembly OD for annulus estimates and flag it explicitly; a detailed bit-junk-slot flow calculation is unavailable.
2. **Tool joints.** The field record identifies 5.5-in pipe with a 4.670-in body bore and 5.5 FH connections, but does not give a connection machining schedule. Use two compatible catalogue geometries as declared scenarios: NOV EIS 7.000-in OD / 4.000-in ID and GPDS 55 7.250-in OD / 3.500-in ID. Neither is asserted to be the installed connection. Use a representative 29-in total restricted length per connection, a stated assumption; also report 0-in and 58-in length sensitivities. Ordinary-pipe joint count is length / (2,378 ft / 75 joints), from the regular-pipe tally on EOW p266; fractional count represents distributed expected count, not an observed tally of each earlier run. The 30 HWDP joints and 913.42-ft length are recorded in each BHA drawing. HWDP connection OD is assigned the same catalogue OD, while its recorded 3.625-in bore is retained through the joint. Unknown internal HWDP machining and centre pads remain limitations.
3. **Local geometric losses.** At every serial BHA bore transition and each representative joint, compute sudden expansion and contraction losses from the area ratios, using the smaller-passage velocity. For area ratio r = A_small / A_large, K_expansion = (1-r)^2 and K_contraction = 0.5(1-r)^0.75; loss = K rho v_small^2 / 2. Use the corresponding annular areas for external joint transitions. Subtract the body friction over the replacement joint length to prevent double counting. Also report zero local-loss scenarios for gradual transitions. These shape scenarios are not rigorous bounds on the as-built string or non-Newtonian restrictions. Check the Reynolds-number applicability of the sudden-change correlation explicitly.
4. **Surface lines.** No as-built high-pressure line schedule or exact pressure-tap position was found in the downloaded reports. Retain all five API RP 13D (2009) surface-equipment cases, with C = 1.00, 0.36, 0.22, 0.15, 0.15, using P_surface = C rho_ppg (Q_gpm/100)^1.86 psi. These are alternative complete connection layouts, not five additive terms. Case 5 is a top-drive layout; the others include a kelly. No layout is selected by pressure agreement. The whole layout may include piping upstream of the Pason pressure tap, so its inclusion is conditional. Report zero-surface-loss accounting separately.
5. **Unresolved tool internals.** HALO includes steering and an MWD pulser. The public manufacturer sheet supplies no operating pressure-loss curve. Float and filter restrictions also have no recorded port/open-area or loss curve. A body ID is insufficient to identify their hydraulic passages. Report the open-passage estimates and numeric pressure per unit additional K at each recorded bore; leave actual proprietary/valve/filter loss unquantified. Zero in a subtotal means no supplied allowance, not evidence of zero loss. API's vendor-curve method cannot supply an absolute number without its reference coefficient. Do not derive any coefficient from the observed SPP or the differential channel.

## Accounting and evidence

Report each sample and each run with measured SPP, every included term, each external increment, accounted pressure/fraction and remaining residual. Residual is measured minus accounted, with no subtraction of the reported differential on motorless runs. Preserve the differential mean and range as a separate diagnostic, including negative values. Summarise every declared geometry/layout scenario; do not select one by residual size. Counts and means weight each frozen sample equally.

The archive will include the executed script, frozen plan and source hashes, model pieces, component and joint estimates, all scenario budgets, per-sample/per-run tables and a methods review. Verify both sums and preservation of the original S1/S2 archives and model files. The estimates do not complete the hydraulic budget where tool internals or surface geometry remain unknown, and they do not identify a cause of the temperature errors.
