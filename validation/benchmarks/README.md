# Circulation and recovery benchmarks

The circulation cases are vertical open holes. Depth is measured down from
the surface. For the matched tests, fluid and rock properties are constant,
heat generated by friction and at the bit is zero, and wall exposure time
is uniform. The analytical and numerical calculations use the same fixed
conductances. These tests check the circulation equations against each other.
A separate calculation with the release physics reports the difference from
the analytical profiles without a pass threshold.

## Kabir circulation

Source: Kabir, Hasan, Kouba and Ameen (1996), SPE Drilling & Completion
11(2), 74–79, [SPE-24581-PA](https://doi.org/10.2118/24581-PA).
For each direction, the numerical integration of the first-order equations
and the closed form must agree to 0.000001 K across both conduits.
The published 15,000 ft example gives a forward annulus maximum at
13,776 ft (p. 76, Figs. 5–6). The paper chose its annulus coefficient
to reproduce the Holmes and Swift steady-state case (p. 77). The
maximum computed with BoreTHM's coefficients is reported separately.

The 6.625 in pipe bore is absent from Kabir's Table 1. The assumed
wall thickness specifies the benchmark geometry. Both cases use BoreTHM's
steel conductivity and the published mud viscosity as a Newtonian constant
for the matched calculation, with zero yield stress. The conductances are
evaluated with the v1.8.0 correlations at the surface pressure and inlet
temperature and fixed there. Kabir's dimensionless formation temperature is
set to the reciprocal of BoreTHM's constant-wall-temperature flux response.
The maximum difference along either conduit must be at most 0.1 K in each
direction. The release calculation uses water-based mud with the stated
surface density and viscosity, variable properties, and native forward
circulation with the case's zero imposed heat sources. Khaled et al.'s
114 °C semisteady temperature (Table 3) and Fig. A-5 provide context
without a pass threshold. [SPE-212550-PA](https://doi.org/10.2118/212550-PA)

## Brown et al. circulation

Brown, Holmes, Zatonski and Toews (2023), [Stanford Geothermal Workshop
paper](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2023/Holmes.pdf),
Table 5 (p. 11) defines both cases. The vertical open hole is 5,000 m deep
and 215.9 mm wide. Pipe ID and OD are 121.4 and 139.7 mm, and pipe and
hole roughness are 0.05 mm. Water enters at 30 °C at 2.5 m³/min and the
annulus outlet has zero gauge back pressure. Formation temperature is
10 °C at surface and increases by 50 °C/km. Rock conductivity is
2.5 W/(m K), density 2,700 kg/m³ and heat capacity 790 J/(kg K).
Penetration rate is 12 m/h. Their calculation uses 500 blocks of 10 m.
The steel and insulated pipe conductive resistances are 0.000508 and
0.01 m K/W, respectively. The insulated case uses steel for the bottom
100 m. The calculation omits casing, cement and explicit BHA losses
(p. 11). BoreTHM uses its cylinder response and its exposure floor on
the time derived from the reported penetration rate. Brown et al.'s
formation resistance uses a Fourier number fitted to an internal
simulation (pp. 6–7); the published description does not supply its value.

The maximum of the circulating fluid temperatures over the whole
well is compared with 178.7 °C (steel) and 73.6 °C (insulated) from
p. 13. The acceptance bands are ±2%, or about 3.6 K and 1.5 K.

## Recovery comparisons

### Venezuelan Well 4

Kutasov and Eppelbaum (2014), [Stanford Geothermal Workshop
paper](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2014/Kutasov.pdf),
pp. 4–5. The vertical well has an 8.5 in hole and 5.5 in casing. The
formation conductivity and diffusivity are 1.46 kcal/(m h K) and
0.0041 m²/h. The diffusivity is used as a positive material property.
The 12,600 ft location circulated for 36 h, with formation and
circulating fluid temperatures of 244 °F and 199 °F. At 10,000 ft
the corresponding values are 756 h, 210 °F and 162.5 °F. Recovery
fraction is (shut-in temperature minus circulating temperature)
divided by (formation temperature minus circulating temperature).
The benchmark uses one minus the dimensionless temperature deficit
in Table 1. The tolerance is ±0.05 in fraction at each time.

### Brown et al. pump-off connection

Use the 5 km insulated case, 260 °C undisturbed rock, and the case 2
transient circulation history in Fig. 17, p. 24. The connection
starts near day 17 in Fig. 21, p. 27. The benchmark is the time
for bottom-hole fluid to reach 150 °C, approximately 233 minutes,
with ±20% tolerance. Figure 21 gives a graphical history; the
paper does not provide a digital initial column profile.

### Khaled et al. FORGE pumps off

Use the FORGE inputs above. Table 3 reports 114 °C before pump-off
and 119, 124, 129, 141, 162, 185, 201, 213, 219 and 220 °C at
5, 10, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 480, 960 and 1,440 minutes respectively.
This is a model comparison with no tolerance. Casing, bottom-hole
assembly and pumps-off film treatment are unspecified. Use the
table values because the fitted Eq. 5 does not reproduce them.
